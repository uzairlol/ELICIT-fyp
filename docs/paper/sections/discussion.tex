Classical reputation theory predicts repair after social stigma \pcite{milinski2002reputation,wedekind2000cooperation}.
The recurring association among SFI agents points in the opposite direction.
Why would social marks precede withdrawal rather than restored giving?

One mechanism is strategic resignation under asymmetric sanction reach.
SFI agents can be named and scored, yet they cannot punish peers in Stage~2 and cannot exit into an enforcing institution.
Once marked, the expected return to repairing standing may look low relative to conserving wealth, especially when marginal per-capita returns are weak.
Withdrawal then becomes a constrained best response rather than a failure of understanding.

A second mechanism is payoff-frame lock-in.
Post-event rationales emphasize marginal returns and self-interest far more often than credibility repair.
That pattern fits calculated-greed accounts of deliberative LLM play \pcite{li2025spontaneous,freeriders2025,rand2012spontaneous}.
Social stigma may increase the salience of individual rationality without activating an image-scoring motive.

A third mechanism is noisy targeting.
Gossip eligibility is nearly ambient because most pairwise scores fall below the bulletin threshold.
Named agents are often relatively high contributors at the hit.
If social marks frequently land on visible givers, agents may update toward ``giving invites scrutiny'' rather than ``giving restores standing.''
Belief labels also over-represent free-rider tags relative to cooperative tags, which can reinforce defensive conservation.

SI agents face a different incentive environment.
They retain Stage-2 tools and higher absolute wealth, so reputation can still function as a coordination or insurance signal in some trajectories.
The sign reversal across replications is consistent with history-dependent evolution under stochastic LLM sampling rather than with a single SI law of motion.
Matched mean intensity between SI and SFI does not erase that difference in response to social marks.

Forced assignment matters for all three mechanisms.
In free-choice baselines, high contributors can flee over-enforcement into SFI.
Here, developed agents cannot exit SI and developing agents cannot acquire Stage-2 reach.
Identification without sanction reach, already visible in mixed baselines, reappears as stigma without repair.

Democratic adaptation and fiscal outcomes sharpen the interpretation.
Both replications expand subsidies, yet only one weakens punishment, so political trajectories diverge under identical whitelists.
Costless meta-governance can rewrite enforcement payoffs without building contribution norms.
Identical shock coverage alongside widening wealth gaps shows that dual-use collection need not equalize stocks when the pool balance is hidden and absolute developed transfers dominate.

Observation and interpretation should stay separate.
The data show a recurring negative association between social marks and next-round intensity among SFI agents, sparse repair language, and divergent SI and democratic trajectories.
The mechanisms above are candidate accounts of that pattern.
They are not uniquely identified by the present design.
