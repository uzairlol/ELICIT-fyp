\subsection{Cooperation levels across seeds}

Table~\ref{tab:coop_summary} summarizes both full-protocol societies.
Mean contribution intensity is 0.293 in Seed~1 and 0.365 in Seed~2.
Within each seed, SI and SFI means are nearly identical.
Agent-mean Mann--Whitney tests do not reject equality of institution means ($p{=}0.817$ and $p{=}0.777$).
Medians remain lower than means, especially for SFI in Seed~1 (0.034 versus mean 0.296).
Seed~2 compresses that median gap (0.205 versus 0.179), yet still shows a thicker SFI zero share (22.4\% versus 11.1\% in SI).
Average cooperation is therefore moderately positive in both societies, while distributions remain right-skewed.

Figure~\ref{fig:prop_cross} overlays round-mean trajectories by institution across seeds.
Seed~2 sits above Seed~1 for most of the horizon.
Pearson correlation of round means across seeds is only 0.194 ($p{=}0.305$).
Institution-specific correlations are likewise weak (SI $r{=}0.144$; SFI $r{=}0.020$).
Mann--Kendall tests find no significant monotonic trend in either seed.
The level shift is a seed-level cooperation increase rather than SI--SFI divergence.
Because institution membership is forced by group, mean parity is descriptive rather than a causal institution effect.

\subsection{Main result: social pressure by institution and seed}

Table~\ref{tab:event_study} and Figure~\ref{fig:rep_cross} report immediate mean $\Delta\mathrm{prop}$ after social-information events.
In both seeds, SFI cells are negative for bad reputation and gossip targeting.
Gossip associations are $-0.201$ (Seed~1) and $-0.084$ (Seed~2).
Bad-reputation associations are $-0.103$ and $-0.069$.
Reputation-drop associations in SFI remain negative, though attenuated in Seed~2.
The developing/non-enforcing withdrawal pattern is the robust core of the study.

SI cells do not replicate in sign.
In Seed~1, SI means after bad reputation, reputation drops, and gossip are negative ($-0.036$, $-0.091$, $-0.048$).
In Seed~2, the same three means are positive ($+0.055$, $+0.058$, $+0.187$).
A universal claim that gossip lowers intensity for all agents is therefore unsupported.
The supported claim is institutional heterogeneity.
Social marks associate with withdrawal among SFI agents across seeds, while SI responses are seed-sensitive.

Opportunistic framing appears 29 times.
Reputation-management language appears 3 times.
Conformity appears 5 times.
Explicit gossip references appear 0 times in the coded sample.

Agents seldom state that they will restore standing by giving more.

\subsection{Shocks, zeros, and composition}

Within-agent post-minus-pre intensity changes around rounds~5 and~10 are not statistically significant by Wilcoxon tests in either seed (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_shock}).
Composition still moves.
Seed~1 shows a sharp SI zero spike after the first shock (41.7\% zeros in round~6), with rationales that cite payoff maximization rather than liquidity constraints.
End-of-round wealth for those agents remains large, so the zeros are strategic rather than liquidity constrained.
Seed~2 shows a more negative mean R5 delta ($-0.080$ versus $+0.023$) and a higher overall SFI zero share.
Mean intensity recovers within one or two rounds in both societies.
Shock--democracy calendar overlap prevents clean attribution of these composition shifts to climate damage alone.

\subsection{Democracy as path-dependent adaptation}

Table~\ref{tab:democracy} lists adopted rules.
Both seeds expand subsidy parameters and raise loss-and-damage equity themes.
Literal round--rule overlap is low; only the final subsidy-fraction theme is shared at round~30.
Seed~1 fails punishment-weakening proposals and instead raises damage weights.
Seed~2 adopts a reduction of punishment effect to 2 in round~20 and ratchets subsidy fraction toward 1.0.
Democratic adaptation is real in both societies and path dependent across seeds.
Proposal rhetoric is portable cooperation boilerplate in both runs.
We treat that divergence as governance context for contribution behavior rather than as a second primary estimand.

\subsection{Loss-and-damage coverage without equalization}

Shock coverage equals 0.768 in both seeds at rounds~5 and~10.
Cumulative payouts remain on the order of $8.5\times10^{5}$, while terminal pools reach $4.34\times10^{9}$ (Seed~1) and $1.30\times10^{10}$ (Seed~2).
Figure~\ref{fig:ldf_cross} shows pool growth.
Final developed--developing wealth gaps widen in both runs and are larger in Seed~2 (Table~\ref{tab:coop_summary}; Figure~\ref{fig:wealth_cross}).
High coverage therefore does not imply stock equalization.
Dual-use deposits grow the pool while absolute developed contributions and endowments continue to dominate wealth trajectories.
Agents do not observe the numeric pool balance, so fund-stock optimization is not a credible behavioral interpretation.

\subsection{Summary of multi-seed evidence}

Three findings survive both independent realizations.
First, SI and SFI mean intensities remain matched within seed while overall levels can shift.
Second, SFI agents reduce intensity after gossip and bad-reputation marks on average.
Third, loss-and-damage coverage coexists with widening wealth gaps.
Two findings are seed-sensitive.
SI responses to social marks reverse sign, and democratic rule paths diverge on punishment weakening.
Opening zero paths and absolute sanction volumes also differ across seeds without overturning the SFI social-pressure association.
The manuscript therefore centers institutional heterogeneity in social pressure rather than a single universal reputation effect.
