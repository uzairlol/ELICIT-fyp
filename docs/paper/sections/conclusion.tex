Reputation and gossip are often assumed to stabilize cooperation in LLM agent societies.
Under climate risk and forced institutional asymmetry, that assumption fails for developing agents in the non-enforcing institution.
Across two independent replications, social marks associate with lower next-round contribution intensity in SFI.
Responses among enforcing-institution agents reverse sign.
Average cooperation remains moderately positive, democratic adaptation is history dependent, and high shock coverage coexists with widening wealth gaps.

The central finding is role-contingent social pressure.
Monitoring without enforceable repair can coincide with withdrawal rather than with restored giving.
Future work should expand replications and models, desynchronize shocks from democracy, restore free institutional choice under climate prompts, and ablate gossip thresholds.
Those extensions can test whether restoring exit or Stage-2 reach converts social stigma back into cooperation technology.
