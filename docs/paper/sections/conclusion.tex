Reputation and gossip are often assumed to stabilize cooperation in LLM agent societies.
Under climate risk, forced institutional asymmetry, and a dual-use loss-and-damage pool, that assumption fails for developing agents in the non-enforcing institution.
Across two independent full-protocol seeds, social marks associate with lower next-round contribution intensity in SFI.
Responses among enforcing-institution agents reverse sign across seeds.
Average cooperation remains moderately positive, democratic adaptation is path dependent, and high shock coverage coexists with widening wealth gaps.

The nugget is institutional heterogeneity in social pressure.
Monitoring without enforceable repair can coincide with withdrawal rather than with restored giving.
Multi-seed evidence is what makes that claim credible.
Future work should expand seeds and models, desynchronize shocks from democracy, restore free institutional choice under climate prompts, and ablate gossip thresholds.
Those extensions can test whether restoring exit or Stage-2 reach converts social stigma back into cooperation technology.
